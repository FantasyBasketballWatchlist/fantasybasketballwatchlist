# Chase timing and price, not better projections

Sharp NBA prop bettors do not mainly win by out-projecting the books from box scores. They win by getting a better **price** (shopping several books, sniping prices that are off the multi-book consensus) and a better **time** (betting before the market absorbs injury and lineup news, or betting against books that update slowly). They prove it with **closing-line value (CLV)**, not win-loss records. Your results fit this picture. A public-data projection model lost about 1.8% at median prices an hour before tip. The books' no-vig probabilities beat it (Brier 0.247 vs 0.255). Almost all of the move to roughly +1% came from best-price shopping and leaning on the market, not from the model's own information. No peer-reviewed study of NBA prop market efficiency exists, so every claim here about where props are soft rests on practitioner evidence and on your own data. The highest-value next steps are cheap. Start collecting your own forward odds snapshots now, so you can measure CLV (the only test your sample size can afford). Re-project at bet time with the latest 15-minute injury report and a 240-minute team budget. Fix two biases already visible in your 2024-25 data: the model projects too low, and it over-reacts to recent minutes. Then test a model-free "consensus outlier" rule. Even if all of that works, the realistic prize is a low single-digit edge at stakes that licensed books will cut to $5–$100 per prop once they notice. Treat this as a research project with a small, capped betting budget, not as an income source.

## Sharps win on price, timing and the consensus, not on box scores

The most consistent message from people who actually beat prop markets is that the model is one input and the market is the base. Unabated, co-founded by leading prop bettor Rufus Peabody, tells bettors to use the market consensus as "one of your blended sources of truth". It weights books that moved recently more heavily because "some books update slower than others, which can create edges against them" ([Unabated](https://unabated.com/articles/introducing-market-based-prop-projections)). Peabody himself reportedly no longer rushes opening numbers. He waits for the market to form, then targets mispriced props, alternate lines and game-script scenarios ([VSiN](https://vsin.com/the-vsin-daily/inside-the-mind-of-sharpest-of-the-super-bowl-prop-sharps/)). Several sources describe props moving "from one of the easiest markets to beat to an efficient market" as betting handle grew ([BetSmart](https://betsmart.beehiiv.com/p/efficient-prop-markets)). The academic evidence closest to your situation points the same way. A single-season NBA study found box-score models lost significantly against the betting market, and only expert video-derived features turned a profit ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331218530_Exploiting_sports-betting_market_using_machine_learning)). Your 2024-25 result is a prop-market replication of that finding.

Props have a structural feature that changes the method: **no single sharp book** anchors them. Pinnacle, Circa and Betcris set game lines but post thin, late prop menus. Practitioners say the "top-down" approach of devigging one sharp book "isn't really possible with props", and they name FanDuel and Caesars as the sharpest US prop originators ([Establish The Run](https://establishtherun.com/understanding-the-current-ecosystem-of-nfl-player-props/)). That source covers NFL props, and Unabated says "the jury is still out" on which books are sharp for props at all ([Unabated](https://unabated.com/articles/introducing-market-based-prop-projections)). Some books share outsourced feeds, so a group of books can move together and look like independent agreement when it is not. The practical benchmark is therefore a **de-vigged consensus across books**, not any one book. Your Brier comparison used median prices across books. It probably understates how sharp the full market is, because a better consensus (power or Shin de-vig, weighted toward the books that move first) would score better still.

The documented soft spots are about time and structure, not projection skill. Openers come out during "price discovery" at low limits, and FanDuel raises limits closer to tip ([Establish The Run](https://establishtherun.com/understanding-the-current-ecosystem-of-nfl-player-props/)). After a late scratch, sportsbooks reprice the backup's prop in about 15 seconds, while pick'em apps can leave it up for minutes ([4for4](https://www.4for4.com/2023/preseason/how-win-money-playing-pick-em-sites)). In one documented case, Lowry and Adebayo lines rose all morning as a teammate's questionable tag spread, and one book still showed the stale number while others had pulled the prop ([RotoGrinders](https://rotogrinders.com/articles/nba-situational-player-projections-dfs-betting-props-analysis-3-9-22-3768105)). The strongest academic evidence that consensus-outlier betting works comes from soccer, not props. Kaunitz, Zhong and Kreiner bet bookmaker odds that stood out from a 32-book consensus. They were profitable in a 10-year backtest and in 5 months of real money, then the books restricted their accounts ([arXiv 1710.02824](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02824)). Press coverage put the returns at about 3.5% in the backtest and 8.5% live ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2017/10/19/67760/the-secret-betting-strategy-that-beats-online-bookmakers/)).

The one structural bias with a plausible mechanism is on **unders**, and it is unproven for NBA props. Prop lines are set "somewhere around a player's median performance" ([Unabated](https://unabated.com/articles/introducing-market-based-prop-projections)). The public prefers overs, and Peabody's team bets mostly unders ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2022/09/28/pop-culture/gamblers-super-bowl-episode-3-rufus-peabody)). The academic analogue, over-betting of NBA game totals, was real but too small to beat the vig ([The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/nba-gambling-inefficiencies-a-second-look/)). The only prop-specific academic work found is an undergraduate thesis on FanDuel NBA closing lines from 2022 to 2025. It found **no consistent over/under bias** ([Claremont](https://scholarship.claremont.edu/cmc_theses/4171)). Take the under idea as a hypothesis to test, not an edge to bet.

## Your 2024-25 data already shows where the model breaks

Your exploratory slices ([analysis/real_lines_explore.json](../analysis/real_lines_explore.json), 14,084 design offers) contain three diagnoses that matter more than any outside source.

First, the model is **biased low**. For points, it gave the over a 44.8% average chance, while overs actually hit 49.2% and the market said 50.1%. Rebounds (46.1% vs 49.0%) and assists (48.0% vs 50.8%) show the same pattern. When the line sat above the player's season average, the model said 39.8% and the outcome was 46.3%. The likely causes are evaluating a symmetric normal at a mean that lags the role the market sees, or a minutes projection that drifts low. This is a calibration bug, not a market insight. It also explains why the model's biggest disagreements were its worst calls: offers where the model and market differed by more than 0.20 had a model Brier of 0.293 against the market's 0.249.

Second, the **3-game minutes half-life over-reacts**. When a player's recent minutes were down 3 or more, overs hit 53.1% while the model said 45.9%. That is the signature of chasing foul trouble, blowouts and one-off rest. Published heuristics are much slower: RotoGrinders uses 75% season and 25% last-5 minutes, an effective window of 10+ games, with explicit adjustments for injuries, role changes and blowouts ([RotoGrinders](https://rotogrinders.com/fantasy/lessons/accurately-predicting-minutes-nba-dfs)). The interesting part is that the **market looks miscalibrated in the same direction, only less so**. On recent-minutes-down offers, the market said 50.2% and outcomes were 53.1% (n=1,788). On recent-minutes-up offers, it said 49.9% and outcomes were 47.6% (n=3,162). When the line sat above the season average, it said 48.0% and outcomes were 46.3% (n=6,117). Each gap is roughly 2.5 standard errors. They all fit the hypothesis that **books over-weight recent form**, and that fits Neil Paine's finding that usage is mostly a stable player trait ([Neil Paine](https://neilpaine.substack.com/p/which-rate-stats-correlate-more-with)). But these are a dozen post-hoc slices of one half-season. Some will be noise. This is the most promising *edge* hypothesis in your own data, and it must be preregistered and tested on fresh seasons before any money follows it.

Third, the **gains from the market blend shrink out of sample**. The blend improved Brier only from 0.2468 to 0.2463 on the design half and from 0.2473 to 0.2471 on the check half. On blended bets with expected value of 5% or more, ROI fell from +9.8% (design) to -3.3% (check). That decay is classic overfitting. In the frozen rule set ([analysis/real_lines_rules_2024-25.json](../analysis/real_lines_rules_2024-25.json)), the same 773 bets lose 17.6% at median prices, so best-price shopping carries the result. Shopping alone cut the two-sided margin from 6.8% to 3.5% ([docs/prereg-real-lines-rules.md](../docs/prereg-real-lines-rules.md)). The injury-news filter (R3) skipped props in **362 of 591 games**. Removing it *raised* reference ROI from +1.0% to +2.7%, and removing the 3% value threshold gave +2.4% on 2,514 bets (interval -1.3% to +6.1%). These are reference numbers on seen data and cannot be used to change the frozen rules. They do say that discarding most of the slate is a crude patch for a missing feature, which is teammate-absence modeling.

You should also know the **pending preregistered test is underpowered**. Two fresh seasons at about 770 bets each give a standard error near 2.5–3% on ROI, so the pass rule needs a realized ROI of roughly 5% or more. A real but realistic 1–3% edge will usually fail it, and a pass is partly luck. The CLV research below explains why. The test also costs credits you do not have: the downloader charges 10 credits per market per game historically, about 18,000 credits for a 590-game window with three markets ([odds_lines.py](../odds_lines.py); [The Odds API](https://the-odds-api.com/liveapi/guides/v4/)).

## CLV is the only scoreboard your sample size can afford

CLV compares the price you bet with the de-vigged closing price. If the close is roughly efficient, CLV is an unbiased estimate of expected value per bet. Joseph Buchdahl puts its per-bet standard deviation at about **0.1, against about 1.0 for profit**, so a 5% CLV edge "might only need 50 bets" while a profit test needs "several thousand" ([Pinnacle Odds Dropper](https://www.pinnacleoddsdropper.com/blog/closing-line-value--clv-demystified-by-expert-joseph-buchdahl)). Applied to your situation, with a two-sided 95% test and 80% power, a profit test needs about **17,800 bets to detect a 2% edge and 7,900 for 3%**. Your one-season sample of about 770 bets has a 95% interval about ±6.7% wide, which is what pure noise produces ([HowProsBet](https://howprosbet.com/how-to-verify-your-betting-edge/) for the method). Two caveats apply to props. Buchdahl's 0.1 figure comes from soccer and Pinnacle prices. Prop lines jump a whole number on news, so prop CLV is noisier, and at a standard deviation of 0.2 a 2% mean CLV needs about 780 bets rather than 200. Buchdahl also warns that CLV is only a proxy and assumes the close reflects the true price. That holds best in deep markets, and NBA props are not one.

The recipe for your data is concrete. Build the benchmark close as the median de-vigged probability across every book quoting the same line at the last snapshot before tip. Use the power or Shin method (for two outcomes, Shin equals the additive method), not the proportional method, which Buchdahl calls "crude and frankly inaccurate" ([implied package](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/implied/vignettes/introduction.html)). Your current blend de-vigs proportionally, which is worth changing. For a matching line, CLV equals the decimal odds you got times the closing fair probability, minus 1. When the line number moves (for example 24.5 to 25.5), fit your negative-binomial or normal to the closing price, solve for the mean, and evaluate it at your bet's line. A one-number move shifts probability by P(X=25), roughly 3–5 points for points props. Report CLV separately for bets where injury news broke between your bet and the close. If all the CLV comes from those bets, it reflects luck or speed, not model skill. Include a placebo: mean CLV across *all* props should be about minus half the vig, and your picks must beat that baseline, not merely zero. Cluster standard errors by game, because points and PRA on one player are nearly the same bet. Finally, run a **calibration check of the close itself**: bin de-vigged closing probabilities against outcomes for every prop. That is free with data you already have, and it tells you whether positive CLV against US-book closes means profit.

The credit math sets the strategy. Historical snapshots cost 10 credits per market per game, so ~1,300 credits buys about 43 game-snapshots of three markets, which is useless for a historical CLV study. Live snapshots cost 1 credit per market per game ([odds_lines.py](../odds_lines.py)). At about 7–8 games a night and three markets, each slate snapshot costs about 22 credits. **This month's 1,300 credits therefore funds about four weeks of forward collection at two snapshots per game**, morning (around 10–11 a.m. ET, before game-day injury reports) and close (tip minus 5–10 minutes), or about 19 days at three snapshots if you keep the T-60 snapshot. The NBA season began October 20, so this is the right moment. That sample (roughly 250 model bets, plus every prop for the placebo and calibration checks) is enough to measure prop CLV's own standard deviation and detect a CLV near 3%. It is also a genuinely fresh, preregistrable test that cannot be contaminated by past tuning. Get game spreads and totals from your ESPN data if it carries them, so you don't spend credits there.

Ranks 2 and 3 matter most for the model. Industry practice treats projected minutes as the foundation and injuries as "the most influential factor" ([RotoGrinders](https://rotogrinders.com/fantasy/lessons/accurately-predicting-minutes-nba-dfs); [Stokastic](https://www.stokastic.com/articles/nba-dfs/nba-dfs-research)). Re-normalizing each team to 240 minutes makes "teammate out, minutes up" fall out automatically. Paine's role-versus-skill result shows *what* moves: assist and rebound rates follow the role (a replacement inherits much of his predecessor's rates), while usage, steals and turnovers stay with the player ([Neil Paine](https://neilpaine.substack.com/p/which-rate-stats-correlate-more-with)). Model this as team-share reallocation with league-pooled role-pair effects, shrunk toward structural priors, because raw with/without splits are tiny and confounded ([squared2020](https://squared2020.com/2017/12/02/usage-and-efficiency/)). The NBA's own schedule gives you the timing. Day-before reports are due at 5 p.m. local and game-day updates between 11 a.m. and 1 p.m. local, and PDFs are posted every 15 minutes ([NBA Official](https://official.nba.com/nba-injury-report)). A fair backtest compares a projection built from the PDF available at time T with the line at time T, never the T-60 line. Your earlier test likely gave the books a head start on news, which alone could explain much of the -1.8%. Be realistic about what this buys. Books set lines with injuries, totals, spreads, pace and back-to-backs in view, so ranks 2, 3 and 6 mostly bring the model to parity. A model at parity plus best-price shopping is roughly where your +1% came from. Real edge, if it exists, will come from rank 1's timing (betting morning lines that later move your way) and from ranks 4 and 5.

Rank 5 deserves a caution and a cheap first look. You can run it now on the 2024-25 reference data at zero credits, because it has essentially one parameter. If it shows nothing there, it will not survive limits live. Be aware that the books it exploits are exactly the ones that limit fastest. Your amendment restricting bets to licensed books was right: about 18% of 2024-25 best prices came from offshore books that are not legal in US states ([docs/prereg-real-lines-rules.md](../docs/prereg-real-lines-rules.md)).

The pick'em route does not rescue a weak model. PrizePicks and Underdog per-leg break-evens run about **54–58%** (57.7% for a 2-pick at 3x, just over 54% for 5- and 6-pick Flex), well above the roughly 52.4% needed at -110 ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/education/prizepicks-flex-play-or-power-play-math)). The historical pick'em edge was sniping fixed lines that had gone stale against sportsbook consensus after news ([4for4](https://www.4for4.com/2023/preseason/how-win-money-playing-pick-em-sites)). Dynamic multipliers erode that edge ([Stokastic](https://www.stokastic.com/articles/dfs-strategy/how-underdog-fantasy-multipliers-work)), and paid pick'em is unavailable in many states ([Saturday Down South](https://www.saturdaydownsouth.com/dfs/legal-states/)). It is only worth trying as an outlet for rank 3 and rank 5 signals in a state where it is legal.

## Stake near zero until CLV says otherwise, and expect the books to cap you

The honest Kelly stake today is about **zero**, because the 2024-25 test cannot reject zero edge. Once forward CLV confirms an edge, size on the CLV-implied or market-shrunk edge, never the raw model edge. Full Kelly at -110 is edge/0.91, about 2.2% of bankroll at a 2% edge. Use **quarter Kelly or less** (about 0.5% at 2%). If your true edge is half your estimate, full Kelly is effectively double Kelly, and long-run growth goes to roughly zero ([Thorp](https://wayback.archive-it.org/5456/20240920155724/https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/cs286r/courses/fall12/papers/Thorpe_KellyCriterion2007.pdf); [MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/docs/kelly-criterion/)). Correlated bets need explicit rules, because the literature treats simultaneous bets as independent ([Grant & Buchen](https://ideas.repec.org/a/buc/jgbeco/v6y2012i2p1-28.html)) and practitioners admit their correlation handling is "not a science at all" ([DataGolf forum](https://forum.datagolf.com/t/bet-sizing-kelly-criterion/43)). Never bet points and PRA (or points+rebounds, points+assists) the same way on one player as separate full stakes. Cap exposure per game at 1–1.5 single stakes and per night at about 10–15% of bankroll. Later, you can estimate the joint covariance of player stats from your box scores and solve a simultaneous Kelly over simulated slates. Plan for variance. With flat stakes at a true 2% edge, a 770-bet season ends in a loss about 29% of the time, and the 90th-percentile drawdown is about 42 units. That is about a 20% drawdown at quarter-Kelly unit sizes, before same-game correlation widens it.

Limits are the binding constraint, not bankroll. Operators say they limit about 1% of customers, and Massachusetts found 0.64% limited as of December 2024. Consistent winners were far more likely to be restricted ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/massachusetts-limiting-sports-betting-debate-sharps-roundtable-september-2024); [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/new-york-legislation-looks-to-prohibit-sportsbooks-from-limiting-bettors-oct-1-2025)). Anecdotal reports put limited prop maximums at **$5–$100**, sometimes within weeks ([agentbets](https://agentbets.ai/guides/draftkings-prop-bets/)). The behaviors that trigger limits (beating the close, betting stale numbers, niche markets) are exactly the ones ranks 1, 3 and 5 aim at. So expect the extractable value per account to depend on how long the account survives, not on bankroll. Since June 2026, Massachusetts requires books to notify limited bettors within about 48 hours and explain which markets are limited, but it does not ban limiting ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/massachusetts-limited-sports-bettors-notice-timely-explain-february-2026)). New York's anti-limiting bill had not passed as of the last report found ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/new-york-legislation-looks-to-prohibit-sportsbooks-from-limiting-bettors-oct-1-2025)). The market itself is shrinking where it was softest. After the 2025 scandals the NBA asked books to scale back props on low-minute players ([Scripps/AP](https://www.scrippsnews.com/sports/nbas-rozier-billups-caught-up-in-fbi-gambling-investigation-reports-say)). On October 16, 2026, the league and major books agreed to remove several prop types, including multi-stat under parlays ([Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/sports/2026/10/16/nba-sportsbooks-agree-to-ban-several-types-of-player-prop-bets-amid-gambling-concerns/)). Your own role-player bets were already the worst slice (-13% on 69 bets).

The no-limit alternatives carry legal risk. Kalshi lists NBA points, rebounds, assists and threes props with a $10,000 per-trade cap ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/kalshi-adds-nba-prop-markets-as-betting-crackdowns-surge/)). ProphetX received CFTC approval in June 2026 and charges a 2% fee on net winnings ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/online-sports-betting/reviews/prophet-exchange)), which eats much of a 1–3% edge. The Third Circuit sided with Kalshi, the Ninth and Sixth Circuits ruled against it, and New Jersey has asked the Supreme Court to settle the split ([Finance Magnates](https://financemagnates.com/fintech/federal-courts-now-disagree-on-kalshis-sports-contracts-new-jersey-wants-the-supreme-court-to-settle-the-fight); [Tennessee Lookout](https://tennesseelookout.com/2026/09/28/federal-court-rules-kalshi-is-subject-to-tennessees-gambling-laws-for-sports-contracts/)). Offshore books stay off the table. Two smaller costs can erase a thin edge. Illinois' per-wager tax led FanDuel, DraftKings and Fanatics to add per-bet surcharges, and even $0.25 is 2.5% of a $10 stake ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/fanatics-illinois-sports-betting-surcharge-july-1-2025)). From tax year 2026, only 90% of gambling losses are deductible, capped at winnings ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/09/02/a-raw-deal-for-gambling-loss-deductions-obbba/168188/)). A high-turnover bettor with a 3% edge can then owe tax on several times their real profit, so keep per-session records from day one.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes the project. The question is no longer "how do we build a better projection than the books?" Public box-score projections are priced in, and your Brier gap confirms it. The question is "can we act on information before the books finish pricing it, and can we prove that cheaply?" The projection model's best role is as the engine for fast re-projection after news, and as a veto on consensus-outlier bets, not as a standalone source of truth. One genuinely new lead came out of this synthesis, from your own data: the market appears to over-weight recent minutes and form, in the same direction as your model but less strongly. If that survives a preregistered forward test, it is the kind of slow, structural mispricing that can persist. If it does not, the realistic ceiling is price shopping plus timing, and the books cap that quickly.

My candid estimate, which is judgment rather than a sourced figure: there is perhaps a one-in-five chance that a model-plus-timing approach shows a reliably positive CLV of 1–3% against a consensus close. Even then, licensed-book limits would probably hold the annual dollar value to hundreds or low thousands per account, not a living. The cheapest way to find out is also the most useful thing to do this week. Spend the remaining 1,300 credits on forward morning and close snapshots, preregister CLV as the primary metric, and fix the low bias and the minutes decay on box scores while the data accumulates. Defer buying historical credits for the frozen 2023-24 and 2025-26 test. As specified, it is unlikely to detect a realistic edge, and a forward CLV result will tell you more for less.
