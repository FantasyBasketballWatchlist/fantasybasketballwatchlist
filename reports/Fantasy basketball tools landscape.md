# ESPN points players are left without an optimizer

The fantasy basketball tools market is mostly three paid specialists sitting on top of free league platforms. **Basketball Monster** ($69.95 a season) is the deepest for in-season analysis and syncs the most platforms. **Hashtag Basketball** ($2.50 a month) is the cheapest, with the best schedule and streaming grids and "what-if" machines that re-rank the whole league. **FantasyPros** ($3.99–$22.99 a month) leads on draft day with live sync and consensus rankings. Under them, **ESPN is free but thin**: its Quick Lineup fills empty slots by ranking, and ESPN itself says its Lineup Protection "is NOT a lineup optimizer." Yahoo sells a lineup optimizer with win probabilities for $39.99–$79.99 a year, but only for Yahoo leagues. The tools are built around categories leagues. Points leagues are supported mostly by swapping in a different scoring setting, and **no tool found advertises an optimizer built for ESPN H2H points**: one that counts only the games that actually fit into your active slots, picks streamers by the starts they add, and gives a calibrated chance of winning this week. That matters for you, because your tested advantages sit in the parts of BPV that apply during the season (injury-report play chances, absence streaks, weekly status factors). They do not sit in preseason rankings, where the FantasyPros consensus picked the top 130 better than your blend in all four backtest seasons (though your formula's point values were more accurate, and an equal-thirds blend with the consensus roughly tied it). The most valuable additions to your app are, in order: read-only ESPN league sync, a daily lineup and streaming optimizer, weekly win probability, and replacement-aware trade and waiver "what-ifs". These are the features where your own tested inputs matter more than a competitor's rankings. One caveat applies throughout. Reddit could not be reached, so user praise and complaints come from app stores, vendor help pages and competitors' own comparisons, and that evidence is thin.

## Three specialists split the paid market, and each wins on something different

The paid sites differ less in raw projections, where none publishes an accuracy record, than in which job they automate. **FantasyPros** sells tiers across all three sports it covers. PRO costs $3.99, $5.99 or $11.99 a month (annual, six-month or monthly billing), MVP $5.99/$8.99/$16.99 and HOF $8.99/$11.99/$22.99, and you can sync 2, 10 or 50 leagues per sport. MVP adds lineup auto-pilot, waiver and trade suggestions and the live-sync draft assistant. HOF adds a "Coach AI," a Waiver Planner, API access and DFS tools ([FantasyPros plans](https://www.fantasypros.com/premium/plans/bp/)). Its NBA suite, My Playbook, offers optimal lineups, a Waiver Assistant, a Trade Analyzer, power rankings and a custom Player Rater ([FantasyPros My Playbook](https://www.fantasypros.com/nba/myplaybook/intro.php)). It also publishes a **separate ESPN-points consensus ranking** ([FantasyPros ESPN points ECR](https://www.fantasypros.com/nba/rankings/overall-points-espn.php)). FantasyPros is a general-purpose sports site, and a competing extension vendor criticizes its basketball advice as "generic start/sit advice." That source is biased ([SportsWZRD](https://www.sportswzrd.com/best-tools/extensions)).

**Basketball Monster** sells one "Monster Membership (H2H/Roto)" for **$69.95 per season**. It syncs Yahoo, ESPN, Fantrax, CBS, Sleeper, Fleaflicker and Ottoneu, the widest list found. Its tools include Draft Monster, a Draft Tracker, Team and Trade Analysis, Analysis Monster (projected final standings that respect game limits and active roster spots), Trade Monster (add/drop suggestions), Matchup Monster, SmartTool, daily projections, a schedule analyzer and defense-vs-position ([BBM membership](https://basketballmonster.com/membership.aspx)). Its FAQ says it handles category or points scoring with custom weights ([BBM help](https://basketballmonster.com/help.aspx)). New for 2026-27 are dark mode, a news phone app, a Chrome extension "for improved ESPN support" and "DURANT Support," a feature the site describes in conflicting ways ([BBM 2026-27 update](https://basketballmonster.com/article.aspx?article=2185)). Two mechanics stand out. The first is **"quality games"**: a game counts as quality only if it falls on a day when fewer league games are played than your game limit (default 5), because on heavy days of 10 or more games, rostered players play but sit on your bench ([BBM help](https://basketballmonster.com/help.aspx)). The second is a trade analyzer that can **fill a player's projected missed games with a likely free agent**, switch between per-game and total value, and "Assume Good Health" ([BBM Trade Analysis](https://basketballmonster.com/TradeAnalysis.aspx)). A competing vendor calls its free draft analyzer the best free one but dense and manual ([Cheatsheet War Room](https://www.cheatsheetwarroom.com/blog/fantasy-basketball/team-draft-analyzers)).

**Hashtag Basketball** is the value pick at **$2.50 a month on Patreon, including a private Discord** ([Hashtag premium](https://hashtagbasketball.com/premium/)). Premium adds full projections, a mock draft simulator, a premium schedule grid that shows waiver players and matchup ranks, waiver rankings, a draft tracker, an H2H Matchup Planner, a Trade Machine, a Waiver Machine, a League Scouting Report, and Beast, Smash, Dynasty and Auction modes. It syncs Yahoo, ESPN (through a Chrome or Firefox extension), Fantrax and Sleeper. Its user guide says Beast, Smash, Trade, Waiver and Scouting support both categories and points on all four platforms, though "availability varies by platform" ([Hashtag user guide](https://hashtagbasketball.com/user-guide)). Its distinctive idea is league-wide simulation: the Trade and Waiver Machines show how a hypothetical move changes **every team's projected power ranking**, and the Matchup Planner lets you toggle who plays on which day and see the projected end of the week ([Hashtag user guide](https://hashtagbasketball.com/user-guide)). It is also one of the few sites with a dedicated points-league ranking page ([Hashtag points rankings](https://hashtagbasketball.com/fantasy-basketball-points-league-rankings)). About 24 of its tools are free, including an advanced schedule grid and a streaming grid color-coded by opponent defense ([Hashtag](https://hashtagbasketball.com/); [Hashtag streaming grid](https://hashtagbasketball.com/fantasy-basketball-streaming-grid)).

The next tier is mostly news, DFS or niche. **RotoWire** costs $14.99 a month ($7.99 billed annually) for season-long, $49.99/$24.99 for daily and $99.99/$39.99 for All Access ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/article/rotowire-premium-subscription-features-benefits-95423)). Its season-long strength is injury status labels and confirmed starters, and its "lineup optimizer" is for DFS ([RotoWire optimizer](https://www.rotowire.com/daily/nba/optimizer.php); [RotoWire injury report](https://www.rotowire.com/basketball/injury-report.php)). **RotoBaller** NBA GOLD costs $19.99 a month or $49.99 a year and includes team sync, a draft kit and a lineup optimizer ([RotoBaller](https://www.rotoballer.com/nba-premium-membership-subscription)). **Razzball** has a category Player Rater (0.00 = average), a schedule grid with pace ratings and a trade analyzer that shows projected stats rather than one opaque number ([Razzball FAQ](https://razzball.com/playerrater-faq/); [Razzball grid](https://basketball.razzball.com/schedule-grid-with-pace-ratings/); [Razzball trade analyzer](https://basketball.razzball.com/trade-analyzer-fantasy-basketball/)). **DARKO** publishes free daily machine-learning projections ([DARKO](https://www.darko.app/about)). FTN, Fantasy Alarm and numberFire lean toward DFS ([FTN](https://ftnfantasy.com/nba/rest-of-season-projections); [Fantasy Alarm](https://www.fantasyalarm.com/pricing); [FanDuel Research](https://www.fanduel.com/research/welcome-to-fanduel-research)). For DFS itself, tools range from free (DailyFantasyFuel) to SaberSim at $97–$297 a month and Stokastic at $199.95–$399.95 a month, and all of them run simulation-based lineup builders aimed at contests ([SaberSim pricing](https://www.sabersim.com/pricing); [Stokastic comparison](https://www.stokastic.com/articles/nfl-dfs/stokastic-sims-vs-sabersim-vs-rotogrinders-nfl-2026)). None of this carries over to your format except the optimization code.

## The platforms themselves add convenience, not analysis, and ESPN adds the least

ESPN's last documented basketball upgrade was in September 2024. It added a draft board showing every pick, practice drafts that use your league's settings, league-manager controls in the app, lineup autosave, and **Quick Lineup**, which fills holes and benches players marked Out ([ESPN 2024 features](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/basketball/story/_/id/41532734/2024-fantasy-basketball-draft-board-new-features-espn)). Quick Lineup decides who starts "using the players' rankings" ([ESPN lineup help](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000958652-Setting-Your-Lineup)), and ESPN's own help page says Lineup Protection "is NOT a lineup optimizer!" ([ESPN Lineup Protection](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/46679515596692-Lineup-Protection)). No 2025-26 or 2026-27 ESPN basketball feature announcement turned up. No evidence was found of ESPN basketball offering win probability, trade suggestions, or a streaming tool based on games remaining. ESPN does treat points leagues as a first-class format, with separate points rankings and tiers every season ([ESPN points rankings 2026-27](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/basketball/story/_/id/49960162/fantasy-basketball-points-league-rankings-2026-27-nba-season)). One detail matters for any optimizer: leagues can choose to **lock lineups at the first game of the week**, which freezes the roster for the whole scoring period "even if the first game falls on a Thursday" ([ESPN lock times](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360054748151-Lineup-and-Roster-Lock-Times)). The ESPN app is rated 4.8 from about 2.1 million ratings across all sports ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/espn-fantasy-sports-more/id555376968)). Complaints collected by a review aggregator center on crashes, hangs while loading the roster, slow updates to position eligibility, and IR players who can't be activated until the next day ([justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/555376968/espn-fantasy-sports-more/reviews)).

Yahoo shows the opposite approach: charge for the analysis. The free tier includes "Start Active Players." Plus costs **$39.99 a year** and Ultra **$79.99 a year** ([Yahoo Plus](https://fantasysports.yahoo.com/lp/plus)). Plus includes Assistant GM, which optimizes lineups with win-probability insights and sends a morning push when a change "could increase your chances to win," plus a Trade Hub that suggests trade partners ([Yahoo Assistant GM](https://sports.yahoo.com/fantasy/article/introducing-assistant-gm-a-smart-new-feature-exclusive-to-yahoo-fantasy-plus-subscribers-125543697.html); [Yahoo Plus benefits](https://sports.yahoo.com/fantasy/article/the-yahoo-fantasy-plus-benefits-program-is-here-see-whats-inside-142137328.html)). The free lineup setter is plain: a player with a game replaces one without, healthy replaces injured, and the result goes stale after later news ([Yahoo help](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN26725.html)). Those prices set the market anchor. People pay **$40–80 a year for in-platform optimization and win probability**, and ESPN users can't buy it from ESPN at any price.

The formats themselves are shifting away from daily lineups. Yahoo's **High Score**, run with the NBA, counts only each starter's best game of the week. It uses PTS 1, REB 1, AST 2, BLK 3, STL 3 with no turnovers, and it became the default for new Yahoo leagues in 2025-26 ([Yahoo High Score](https://sports.yahoo.com/fantasy/article/high-score-101-yahoos-new-default-fantasy-basketball-format-has-arrived--heres-everything-to-know-160017444.html)). Sleeper's Lock-In mode counts one chosen game per player per week ([RotoWire on Lock-In](https://www.rotowire.com/basketball/article/nba-fantasy-how-to-play-sleepers-new-lock-in-mode-75705)). Both formats make streaming worth much less. ESPN H2H points has not moved that way, so for your format **games played and daily lineup decisions still drive results**. That is the same thing your backtests found: availability and minutes dominate error.

The commissioner platforms charge per league for customization, not analysis. Fantrax Premium costs $129.95 per league-season and adds salaries and contracts ([Fantrax](https://www.fantrax.com/newui/premiumLeague.go?gameId=600)). CBS Commissioner costs $149.99 a season ([CBS FAQ](https://www.cbssports.com/fantasy/basketball/games/commissioner/frequently-asked-questions)). Sleeper supports snake, linear and auction drafts, with mock boards that accept custom ADP ([Sleeper](https://sleeper.com/fantasy-basketball)).

## League sync is the moat, and ESPN sync always runs through cookies or an extension

Every serious tool gets its value from knowing your roster, your opponents and your free agents. For ESPN that access is informal everywhere. FantasyPros' live ESPN draft sync needs its **Chrome extension, with the draft room and the assistant open in two tabs, and it does not work on mobile** ([FantasyPros support](https://support.fantasypros.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001362308-How-do-I-launch-the-Draft-Assistant-w-Sync-to-connect-to-my-draft); [FantasyPros mobile ESPN](https://support.fantasypros.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004245367)). FantasyPros keeps a help article for the "Waiting for draft sync..." error ([FantasyPros support](https://support.fantasypros.com/hc/en-us/articles/28876394605339--Waiting-for-draft-sync-error-message-how-to-fix-during-a-live-draft-ESPN-Yahoo-Sleeper-etc)). Hashtag needs an extension for ESPN, and Basketball Monster just shipped one "for improved ESPN support." The reason is that ESPN has no OAuth and no developer program. Private leagues are read with the user's `espn_s2` and `SWID` cookies, which expire, through the unofficial v3 API, which now lives at `lm-api-reads.fantasy.espn.com` because the old host redirects to HTML ([espn-api wiki](https://github.com/cwendt94/espn-api/wiki); [sleeperdynasty PR](https://github.com/calvinlmickens/sleeperdynasty/pull/4)). The MIT-licensed `espn-api` library (about 981 stars) already wraps basketball free agents, box scores, scoreboards and the full schedule. Its tests hit live ESPN endpoints, so it breaks when ESPN changes things ([espn-api](https://github.com/cwendt94/espn-api); [Basketball intro](https://github.com/cwendt94/espn-api/wiki/Basketball-Intro)). Your own app already reads ESPN's `kona_player_info` view from that host.

The other platforms rank from easy to hard. **Sleeper** is easiest: a free, read-only API with no token. Its docs say NFL only, but its NBA endpoints returned live 2026 data in a test run for this research, and commercial use needs a license ([Sleeper API](https://docs.sleeper.com/)). **Yahoo** now requires a **hand-reviewed application** on top of OAuth 2.0 and is **read-only** ([Yahoo developer access](https://sports.yahoo.com/developer/access/)). Fantrax works through an unofficial wrapper that needs a login cookie, and CBS's API is deprecated ([FantraxAPI](https://github.com/meisnate12/FantraxAPI); [CBS token fetcher](https://github.com/geoffharcourt/cbs_fantasy_sports_api_token_fetcher)). **No platform gives third parties write access**, so every tool can only recommend adds and lineups. Setting them automatically on ESPN takes browser automation: the paid "Pro" tier of the WZRD extension, the "ESPN NBA Auto Setup" extension or scripts like fntsylu ([WZRD](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fantasy-basketball-wzrd/bmojbnihkmbdandkddobjnilkegcooll); [Auto Setup](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/espn-nba-fantasy-team-aut/nmehekgchlioodlggejkfhiglajaonie?hl=en); [fntsylu](https://github.com/michael-langaman/fntsylu)).

The browser extension category is small. **WZRD** overlays Yahoo and ESPN pages with a weekly matchup projection (score only), games remaining and a schedule grid. Its Pro tier adds projections from 7/14/30-day or season averages, "Me vs. the League" and ESPN auto-start. It has about 5,000 users and a 4.5 rating from 72 reviews, and its vendor admits the projections are rest-of-season rather than matchup-specific ([WZRD](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fantasy-basketball-wzrd/bmojbnihkmbdandkddobjnilkegcooll); [SportsWZRD](https://www.sportswzrd.com/best-tools/extensions)). The existence of several ESPN auto-start hacks is the clearest sign of unmet demand in this research. **Community code** fills the "my own league" gap, but it is fragile. Most end-user apps have 0–34 stars and are often abandoned. One example is wcrasta's ESPN categories tool, unmaintained since April 2023 ([wcrasta](https://github.com/wcrasta/ESPN-Fantasy-Basketball)). The reusable pieces are giasemidis' ESPN matchup Monte Carlo, which draws counting stats from a Poisson distribution and percentages from a Gaussian, over 100,000 simulations ([giasemidis](https://github.com/giasemidis/espn-nba-fantasy)); an MLB win-probability simulator with per-stat overdispersion, seeded from live ESPN state ([fantasy-wp](https://github.com/mpozar/fantasy-wp)); and the MIT `pydfs-lineup-optimizer` for integer-programming lineups ([pydfs](https://github.com/DimaKudosh/pydfs-lineup-optimizer)). No open-source NBA streaming or schedule optimizer was found.

## What the best draft and in-season tools actually compute

On draft day the valued features, judging by what vendors put behind paywalls, are automatic tracking of taken players, need-based suggestions, and **the percent chance a player lasts to your next pick**. FantasyPros' Pick Predictor looks one to three rounds ahead and grades your draft with projected standings and "steals and reaches" ([FantasyPros Draft Wizard](https://draftwizard.fantasypros.com/basketball/draft-software/); [FantasyPros blog](https://blog.fantasypros.com/08-22-2023-making-the-most-of-the-mock-draft-simulator/)). Its bots draw from many expert cheat sheets and add logic for team needs, rather than following ADP ([FantasyPros NFL draft software](https://draftwizard.fantasypros.com/football/draft-software/)). The cheap version is a normal curve on ADP: availability = 1 − Φ((next pick − ADP)/σ), with σ ≈ ADP/4 when no spread is published ([DraftKick](https://draftkick.com/blog/projected-availability/); [FanGraphs workbook](https://fantasy.fangraphs.com/adp-availability-workbook/)). Basketball Monster's DynV re-values players as their stats leave the pool, and its Draft Tracker syncs live with Yahoo, ESPN and CBS ([BBM tutorials](https://basketballmonster.com/drafttrackertutorials.aspx)). For points leagues, the strategy writing agrees that raw point accumulation and games played matter most ([Fantrax HQ](https://fantraxhq.com/fantasy-basketball-points-league-strategy-and-draft-guide-how-to-outsmart-your-league-mates/); [ESPN points strategy](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/basketball/story/_/id/20631092/fantasy-basketball-points-league-strategy)).

During the season, the best mechanics are Basketball Monster's quality-games counting, its SmartTool's "estimated wins/losses vs. your opponent" with add/drop suggestions to raise your odds ([BBM](https://basketballmonster.com/questionsanswered.aspx)), Hashtag's day-by-day Matchup Planner and league-wide machines, and BBM's replacement fill in trades. **No vendor publishes how it computes win probability, or how well calibrated it is**, and none publishes a formula for ranking streamers. For H2H points, the right formula follows from the slot constraints. A streamer's weekly value is the sum, over his game days, of how much he beats the worst starter he would push out that day, counting zero on days he would sit on your bench. That is stricter than "games × points per game," and it generalizes BBM's quality-games idea from a league-wide game count to your own roster. Win probability in points is simpler than in categories. The weekly margin is roughly normal, so banked points plus the projected difference, divided by the spread of that difference, gives the odds. Simulation is needed only for categories.

## Conclusion

The market has specialized by scoring culture, not by skill. Categories players are well served by cheap tools with plenty of features, while ESPN points players get only a rankings-based lineup fill and pay for sync that runs through browser extensions. The opening is not better preseason rankings: your own tests show consensus wins there, and no competitor publishes accuracy anyway. The opening is to turn the in-season model you have already validated into daily decisions: who starts tonight, whom to stream, and what your chance is of winning this week. Those are the questions where availability and minutes decide outcomes, which is where your tested BPV components add value.

One more advantage is easy to miss. Every tool in this market asks you to trust its numbers without evidence. None, from FantasyPros to Basketball Monster to Yahoo, publishes accuracy or calibration. Your app already runs an accuracy tracker and saves snapshots automatically. If you extend that to win-probability calibration and to the lineup gains you actually realize, that becomes a feature no one else offers. It is also the honest way to find out whether items 2 and 3 help you win, rather than assuming they do.
